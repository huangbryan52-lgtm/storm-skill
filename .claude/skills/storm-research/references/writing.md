# Stage 4 — Write

Draft the article section by section, following the outline. Write each section
**only from the sources mapped to it** in `outline.md`. The lead section is
written later, in polish.

## Rules

- **Grounded, not recalled.** Every factual, non-obvious claim must trace to a
  collected source. If you find yourself asserting something with no source
  row, stop: either search for a source and add it to `sources.md`, or cut the
  claim. Do not fill gaps from memory.
- **Cite inline.** Attach source IDs to the claims they support, e.g.
  "STORM generates questions from multiple perspectives [S1]." Place the
  citation right after the supported claim, not just at paragraph ends.
- **One idea, right citation.** When a sentence draws on two sources, cite both
  `[S1][S4]`. Don't blanket a paragraph with every ID.
- **Neutral, encyclopedic tone** by default (match the user's requested tone if
  different). Report what sources say; attribute opinions and contested claims
  ("Critics argue … [S6]").
- **Present conflicts honestly.** Where sources disagree, say so and cite both
  sides rather than silently picking one.
- **No fabricated specifics.** Dates, numbers, names, and quotes must come from
  a source. If a source is vague, stay vague — don't sharpen a claim beyond
  what it supports.

## Procedure

For each section in outline order:

1. Pull up the mapped source rows from `sources.md`.
2. Write the prose synthesizing those sources — paraphrase, don't copy. Short
   direct quotes are fine when wording matters; mark them as quotes and cite.
3. Attach citations as you go.
4. If you hit a claim with no backing source, flag it inline with
   `[CITATION NEEDED]` and either resolve it before finishing the section or
   note it as a gap. Never leave `[CITATION NEEDED]` in the final deliverable —
   resolve or cut it in polish.
5. Keep section length proportional to the depth of its sourcing and the
   confirmed overall length.

## Style guidance

- Prefer clear topic sentences; put the main claim first, support after.
- Use subsections and lists where they aid scanning, prose where argument
  needs to flow.
- Define jargon on first use for the confirmed audience.
- Avoid filler ("It is important to note that…") and unsupported superlatives.

When every section is drafted and passes the writing gate in `workflow.md`,
move to polish.
